Plain English

Not sure which form you need?

The names are trade shorthand. Here is what each one actually is.

FormWhat it isChoose it when
Circle A round blank cut from sheet You are pressing, spinning or deep-drawing a round part — a vessel, a cap, a base
Coil Continuous metal wound on a core You feed a press or roll-former continuously and do not want to stop to reload
Sheet Flat cut lengths, roughly 0.3–6 mm You cut, bend or fabricate individual flat pieces
Strip Narrow material slit from wide coil You need a specific narrow width — contacts, clips, banding, terminals
Plate Heavy gauge, above roughly 6 mm You are machining it, or you need mass and conductivity — busbars, wear plates
Foil Very thin material, under 0.1 mm You are shielding, laminating, shimming or wrapping

Copper or brass?

Copper conducts electricity and heat better than almost anything else, and it draws beautifully. Choose it for electrical work, heat transfer and deep-drawn vessels.

Brass is copper with zinc added. It is harder, machines far better, costs less, and holds a spring temper. Choose it for hardware, fittings, contacts and anything that gets machined.

Which temper?

Soft / annealed bends and draws without cracking — use it for forming.

Half hard is the general-purpose middle ground: it holds shape but still bends.

Full hard / spring resists deformation and springs back — use it for clips and contacts, but do not expect to form it.

Not sure? Send the part drawing and we will suggest a temper.
